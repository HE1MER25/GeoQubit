# UIQC Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 — Project Judging Criteria

## Purpose

This rubric guides judges in evaluating final team projects and selecting the **top three submissions**. It applies to beginner, intermediate, and advanced projects. Assess demonstrated understanding and quality of execution rather than project difficulty alone.

## Evaluation Rubric (100 Points)

| Criterion | Maximum Points | What to Assess |
|---|---:|---|
| **1. Conceptual Understanding** | **25** | Accurate explanation of quantum computing concepts, algorithms, and relevant mathematics; ability to justify methodological choices. |
| **2. Implementation and Correctness** | **25** | Correct and functional Qiskit implementation; appropriate methods; reproducibility and evidence that code runs as described. |
| **3. Results and Interpretation** | **20** | Clear results, suitable validation or benchmarks, sound interpretation, and acknowledgment of limitations. |
| **4. Problem-Solving and Independent Contribution** | **15** | Evidence of experimentation, debugging, critical thinking, and meaningful work beyond simply reproducing provided notebooks. |
| **5. Presentation and Documentation** | **10** | Clear, well-organized report and figures; usable repository documentation; compliance with the six-page submission limit. |
| **6. Responsible AI Use and Collaboration** | **5** | Transparent declaration of AI, mentorship, and reference assistance; demonstrated ownership of work and contributions from both teammates. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Rating Scale

For **each criterion**, assign a rating from **0 to 5**:

| Rating | Description |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Excellent |
| 4 | Very good |
| 3 | Good |
| 2 | Fair |
| 1 | Poor |
| 0 | Not demonstrated |

Calculate points for each criterion using:

**Criterion points = (rating ÷ 5) × maximum points**

Add the six criterion scores to obtain a total out of **100**.

## Selection of the Top Three

1. Each judge independently reviews every submitted report and its linked GitHub repository using this rubric.
2. Calculate the **mean total score across judges** for each team.
3. Rank teams by mean score; the three highest-scoring teams are provisionally selected.
4. Resolve ties using, in order: **Conceptual Understanding**, **Implementation and Correctness**, then **Results and Interpretation** (comparing mean criterion scores across judges).
5. Where practical, conduct a brief **5-minute demonstration or Q&A** with shortlisted teams to verify understanding and individual contributions before confirming awards. This is a verification step, not an additional scored criterion; any score changes should be justified against the same rubric and documented.

## Fairness and Integrity Notes

- **Do not award points solely for greater project complexity.** A well-executed beginner project may outperform an incomplete advanced project.
- **AI use is permitted.** Assess whether participants understand and can verify AI-assisted work, not whether they used AI at all.
- **Check the assistance declaration.** The event requires disclosure of AI tools, references, external materials, mentorship, and how they were used.
- **Use the same evidence standard for all teams.** If a notebook cannot run because of an external service or account limitation, consider the provided outputs, code, and documented validation; do not assume successful execution without evidence.
- **Declare conflicts of interest.** A judge who mentored or otherwise has a material connection to a team should disclose this to the organizing committee before evaluating that team.
